# Architecture

`stress_cam.py` runs the OpenCV capture, MediaPipe inference, feature extraction, legacy scoring, presentation, and UDP publication in one loop. `face_features.py` runs the Face Landmarker in video mode, with one face, blendshapes, and a facial transformation matrix. `eye_tracking.py` consumes the same pixel landmarks and pitch/yaw/roll estimate. `pulse.py` still supplies optional experimental rPPG. The existing feature order in `stress_model.py` is retained for recordings and saved personal models. The source contains no DeepFace or standalone TensorFlow inference. MediaPipe can use an internal TensorFlow Lite delegate.

The eye tracker collects open-eye samples during center calibration and uses medians for iris position, EAR and head orientation. After calibration, blink closure and reopening use hysteresis at 60% and 68% of open EAR. Closures of 60–450 ms count as blinks. The center band is ±0.07 eye widths horizontally and ±0.055 vertically with pitch and yaw within ±15 degrees of the reference. These are engineering defaults. Stability resets when iris offset changes by more than 0.035 eye widths from its anchor. Tracking gaps over 200 ms break continuity. A detected face can yield usable EAR while orientation is uncertain. A missing frame emits a failure packet when UDP is due.

The rolling proxy weights observations by elapsed usable time. It excludes closes and gaps from the orientation denominator and reports coverage. The camera loop avoids extra inference and sends at most 20 datagrams per source second. DNS resolves once at startup and transmission uses a nonblocking socket. The existing video path consumes each frame in order. Webcam backend buffering and hardware drops have no application-level instrumentation beyond failed reads. Preview and scoring still run on the same thread.

## Calibration and human evaluation

1. Keep the display and camera stationary. Sit at the playing distance, face the game, and fixate a center target during eye calibration. Use **c** after adjusting the display or seating. Eye calibration runs independently of the legacy stress baseline.
2. Present randomized center/edge/off-screen targets with onset timestamps. Include reading or thinking blocks in which a player deliberately looks away. Repeat with glasses, low light, and small head rotations. Record target labels, packet timestamps and validity, and opt-in blink annotations. Keep the original frames local if they are needed for annotation.
3. Compare center-oriented events against center-target labels, report valid coverage and a confusion matrix, and measure blink precision/recall against annotations with a declared temporal tolerance. Report medians and tail latency, stratify by participant and setup, and repeat on a later session to estimate drift.
4. Use a separate multi-point calibration and held-out targets before enabling display coordinates. Measure error in display units or degrees of visual angle with the corresponding viewing geometry. Estimate end-to-end delay with a timed stimulus and receipt log, or a hardware-backed capture timing reference if sensor latency matters.
5. For a stress model, collect session-level and event-level labels using a consented, independently measured protocol. Include controls for task difficulty, fatigue, arousal, speech, and lighting. Compare to simple baselines and hold out entire sessions and participants. Existing self-labeled recordings establish correspondence to those sessions. They cannot establish a validated psychological stress measurement.
